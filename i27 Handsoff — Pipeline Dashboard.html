    @Max Norris — I've run the Lumen Lite vs full Lumen profiling for you on L008 while it was free. Numbers below, but please read the first section first — it explains why your own measurements probably weren't moving.

    ## ⚠️ The project is capped at 45 fps, and that's not a performance result

    In Config/DefaultEngine.ini:
    ```ini
    bSmoothFrameRate=True
    SmoothedFrameRateRange=(LowerBound=(Inclusive,44.0), UpperBound=(Exclusive,45.0))
    ```

    Unreal is smoothing the frame rate to 44–45 fps. With that on, I measured three completely different lighting configurations and got 22.23 ms every single time — identical to two decimal places. Lumen Lite genuinely reduced GPU work, and the frame rate did not move at all, because the engine just idled the extra time away.

    So if you've been changing lighting settings and watching the frame rate stay flat — that's why. It wasn't your changes. Nothing you did to the lights could have moved that number.

    It also means your 60fps target can't be observed in this project as configured, since the engine won't go above 45. Worth raising with Dan before anything else — I haven't changed the setting, I only overrode it for measurement.

    ## The real numbers (cap disabled, 1920×1080, packaged build, on L008)

    | | Full Lumen | Lumen Lite |
    |---|---|---|
    | fps | 64.9 | 69.8 |
    | Frame time | 15.41 ms | 14.32 ms |
    | GPU time | 14.03 ms | 12.77 ms |
    | Render thread | 15.40 ms | 14.31 ms |
    | Game thread | 4.42 ms | 4.30 ms |

    Lumen Lite is worth ~4.9 fps (+7.6%) and saves 1.26 ms of GPU (−9.0%). A real, modest win — and it supports your brief's choice of Lumen Lite.

    Also worth noting: the scene already runs at 64.9 fps at 1080p on a laptop RTX 4060 with full Lumen. That's a decent starting position for a 60fps target.

    ## Two things to be careful about

    1. Your bottleneck is the render thread, not the GPU. RenderThreadTime (15.40 ms) is almost exactly FrameTime (15.41 ms), with GPU just behind at 14.03 ms. They're close, so GPU savings do help — but the render thread is what's setting the pace. That's about draw calls and what the renderer is asked to submit, not shader cost.

    2. MegaLights costs ~0.1 ms at the spawn camera — essentially nothing. I measured it on and off and the difference was inside the noise. That almost certainly means there are barely any dynamic lights in view where the character starts. Since your whole card is about lanterns and torches casting dynamic shadows, this camera position isn't testing your feature. Any before/after you capture from spawn will show roughly nothing regardless of what you do.

    If you want numbers that mean something for your brief, you need to profile standing somewhere with a lot of your lanterns visible. That's the single most useful change you could make to your method.

    ## How I got these (worth copying)

    Profile the packaged build, not the editor — editor frame times include editor overhead and aren't representative.

    ```
    JIMA.exe -windowed
     -ini:Engine:[/Script/Engine.Engine]:bSmoothFrameRate=False
     -ExecCmds="r.SetRes 1920x1080w"
     -ForceDPCVars="r.Lumen.FinalGatherMethod=1,t.MaxFPS=0"
     -csvCaptureFrames=900
    ```
    • bSmoothFrameRate=False removes the 45 fps cap
    • r.SetRes 1920x1080w forces the resolution — check it worked, mine silently rendered at 1423×889 until I forced it
    • r.Lumen.FinalGatherMethod — 1 = full Lumen, 0 = Lumen Lite
    • -csvCaptureFrames=900 writes a CSV to Saved/Profiling/CSV/ you can open in Excel

    The CSVs from all my runs are on L008 in that folder if you want the raw per-frame data for your GPU-profiling deliverable.

    ## On scope

    Your card asks for a MegaLights + Lumen Lite lighting profile and GPU frame-time breakdowns before/after relighting. Engine-wide optimisation work — garbage collection, memory tuning and so on — isn't in your brief, and the numbers above suggest it wouldn't help anyway: your frame is lighting and render-thread bound, and the game thread is only 4.3 ms of a 14.3 ms frame.

    The one item still unticked on your card — "Maximum Light Budget Agreement: an agreed upper cap on active dynamic shadow casters on screen" — is, I'd suggest, the most valuable thing left. You can't lock a profile that holds a frame-rate target without knowing how many shadow-casting lights are allowed on screen.

    Caveats, so you don't over-claim in your write-up: this is a laptop RTX 4060 at 1080p, static camera at player spawn — it shows the shape of the costs, not a PS5 result. It cannot answer "does this hold 60 fps on PS5" on its own.
